Description :

Yanni’s 1997 “Tribute” concert was a historic musical project that made him the first Western musician to perform at two of the world’s most iconic landmarks: the Taj Mahal in India and the Forbidden City in China. The live album and video of the same name, released later that year, documented these extraordinary performances.

🎵 Concert Background and Significance
These concerts were groundbreaking. At the Taj Mahal, the event marked the first time in nearly four centuries that the monument was illuminated at night specifically for a concert. Subsequently, in Beijing, Yanni became the first foreign artist permitted to hold a concert within the Forbidden City. Yanni described the project as a tribute not only to the builders of these architectural marvels but also to the people of India and China.

🎼 Musical Style and Performance
The performances featured Yanni on keyboards leading an ensemble that blended his signature New Age style with orchestral and world music elements. He was accompanied by a full band, orchestra, vocalists, and a choir, utilizing a diverse array of instruments including violin, didgeridoo, duduk, charango, and various flutes. The repertoire consisted of new compositions written specifically for these events, such as “Deliverance,” “Tribute,” and “Love Is All,” alongside other pieces.

📀 Release and Reception
The concert was released as a live album in November 1997 and as a video (directed by George Veras) in December 1997. The video version, narrated by Christopher Plummer, also included a documentary segment titled “No Borders, No Boundaries”. The album was a commercial success, achieving Platinum certification from the RIAA in March 1998.
